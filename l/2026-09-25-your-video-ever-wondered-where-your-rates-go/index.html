Open letter: your video “Ever wondered where your rates go?” — questions on content, framing and authority

Dear Mr Constance,

Council has published a video in which you set out what residents receive for their rates, closing with the words “I beg to differ … you do get value for your money.”

Most of the activity figures you cite are real.

The difficulty is what the video counts as rates-funded, what it leaves out, and who is speaking for Council on a question of value for money.

What the video includes that rates do not pay for
· Shoalhaven Water, the waste transfer stations and Holiday Haven are presented as part of what rates deliver.

  • Water and sewer are funded by separate water and sewer charges (Water and Sewer Services, $92.7 million in 2024–25).
  • Waste services are funded by waste management charges, which under s 496 of the Local Government Act 1993 may not exceed the reasonable cost of the service.
  • Holiday Haven is a commercial undertaking. Including them in a video about rates gives residents an inflated picture of what their general rates buy.

What the video leaves out
The video gives no dollar figure for any service. It does not mention:

  • the road renewal backlog of more than $280 million identified in Council's own 2025 Road Condition Report (133 km very poor, 95 km poor, 570 km needing resurfacing) — a figure you described publicly in April 2026 as evidence the system is “unsustainable”;
  • that the aquatic centres and the Shoalhaven Entertainment Centre operate at a loss — about $4.6 million and $1.9 million a year respectively, according to Council's own 2023 SRV FAQs;
  • the July 2024 reductions in pool seasons and library, leisure centre and waste depot hours, or the CISP 2056 proposal to consult on closing Milton Library;
  • the NSW Audit Office's finding (Local Government 2024) that Shoalhaven had not met any of the three financial benchmarks for at least three years; or
  • your own email of 16 September 2026 to community forum invitees referring to “significant financial pressures” and “difficult decisions”.

A resident watching the video would not know any of this.

That is why I say it tells only part of the story.

Who speaks for Council on value for money
Section 226(c) of the Act makes the Mayor “the principal member and spokesperson of the governing body, including representing the views of the council as to its local priorities”.

Section 335(a) gives the general manager the day-to-day management of the council. Describing services is operational.

Telling residents that the level of rates represents value for money is a judgement about Council's priorities and performance, and it comes at a time when Council has flagged the potential need for a future special rate variation.

  • 320,000 library visitors;

  • 16,000 learn-to-swim classes;
  • 140,000 customer interactions (how many of these were complaints to staff);
  • 450 km of stormwater network;
  • 90 km of open drainage; and Shoalhaven Water as the “fourth largest water utility in New South Wales”?
