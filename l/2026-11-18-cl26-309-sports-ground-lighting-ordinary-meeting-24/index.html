OPEN LETTER
To: All Councillors, Shoalhaven City Council; Mr Andrew Constance, Chief Executive Officer
From: Stephen Prothero, Eye on Shoalhaven Council
Date: 18 November 2026
Subject: CL26.309 Sports Ground Lighting (Ordinary Meeting, 24 November 2026): vote against, or send it back until it is done properly
STATUS OF THIS LETTER
This is a public open letter to the Chief Executive Officer. It is published by Eye on Shoalhaven Council as a matter of public interest journalism.
It is not a complaint. It is not a complaint under Council's Complaint Handling Policy, and it is not to be registered, assessed or managed as one.
It is not a Code of Conduct complaint. It does not allege a breach of the Code of Conduct by any person, and it is not to be referred to the Complaints Coordinator, a conduct reviewer or the Office of Local Government.
It is not an access application. It is not an application under the Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW), formal or informal, and no statutory time limit is started by it.
It is not a request for service. It does not ask Council to take action, to make a decision, or to review a decision.
It is a set of questions put to a public official in the same way a journalist puts questions to the head of an organisation. The Chief Executive may answer all, some or none of the questions, and may do so in whatever form and at whatever time he chooses. Some questions can be answered in a line and others may need work. If the Chief Executive intends to comment on any question, a short reply to this email saying so is all that is needed.
Replies will be published in full. If no reply is received, that will be recorded as it stands.
Dear Councillors and Mr Constance,
Item CL26.309 asks Council to rescind MIN18.271 and reinstate floodlighting fees for sporting clubs from the April 2027 winter season. I ask Councillors to vote against it. If you will not do that, I ask you at the least to send it back until it is properly done. This is a very poor paper, and the reasons are set out below.
1. The election commitment
The Shoalhaven Independents 2024 election flyer for Wards 1, 2 and 3 was headed "Patricia White & Shoalhaven Independents have a PLAN". Among its commitments it said, in plain words:
"No Charge for SPORT or 'User-pays'"
It also said "Residents voices first and foremost – Residents will be heard". The flyer asked voters to Vote 1 Patricia White for Mayor and was authorised by her.
This paper asks Council to charge sporting clubs. It does not mention the commitment.
I ask each of the following to stand by what was put to voters, or to explain publicly to the clubs and families who voted on it why they will not:
• The Mayor, Cr Patricia White, whose name and authorisation are on the flyer. You voted for waiving floodlight charges in September 2018 (SA18.216) and for re-endorsing no charge for community sporting groups in February 2023 (MIN23.43).
• SIG: Crs Luciano Casmiri, Denise Kemp, Bob Proudfoot and Peter Wilkins. Cr Proudfoot, you moved the amendment in 2018 that waived the fees and called the proposed charge unfair.
• The councillors who have left SIG: Cr Karlee Dunn and Cr Steele, together with the Mayor. To the extent you stood as a Shoalhaven Independents candidate in 2024, the commitment was made on your ticket.
Whether you remain in SIG or have since left it, voters were not told that the promise had an expiry date or an exception for cost recovery. I will record how each of you votes.
2. Why this is a very poor paper
1. It rescinds the wrong decision. MIN18.271 withdrew invoices in 2017-18 and has been carried out. The live decisions are MIN23.43 (13 February 2023, carried 9-4), which re-endorsed not charging community sporting groups and said Council continues to cover the cost of lighting, and Sportsgrounds Management Policy POL18/70 clause 3.2.b. The paper does not mention either, and does not recommend amending them.
2. It gets the order of the process wrong. Recommendation 2 reinstates the fees, and Recommendation 4 then exhibits them for 28 days. Section 610F(1) of the Local Government Act 1993 provides that a council must not determine the amount of a fee until it has given public notice and considered submissions. Fees cannot be decided first and exhibited afterwards.
3. The money is small and not tested. The fee recovers about $29,480 a year, which is electricity only. The paper's headline $140,680 "subsidy" includes $111,200 of maintenance that the fee does not recover. The paper does not say what exemptions remove, what Bookable administration, invoicing and bad debts cost, or whether the 16 section 355 committee sites are included and who keeps the revenue.
4. The grant argument is asserted, not shown. The paper says the saving will help Council co-contribute to grant-funded renewal, but names no grant, no amount and no co-contribution. Grants for lighting need co-contributions of 25% to 100%, so $29,480 a year does not obviously unlock anything. It does not mention Faster Assessments Round 2 or Better Open Spaces.
5. It promises benchmarking against comparable councils and shows none.
6. It offers no analysis of what a club, or a junior team, would pay across a season, and no assessment of participation or volunteer burden.
7. It consults clubs only after Council has endorsed the fees. That is the opposite of "Residents will be heard".
8. It leans on the Community Infrastructure Strategic Plan, but that plan says only to "investigate" cost recovery and differentiated pricing for community and commercial users. The 78% support figure covers the sports recommendations as a whole, not a charge on users.
9. It tells the 2018 story selectively. It omits the 2017-18 reprieve, the 2022 policy wording and the 2023 re-endorsement, so Councillors are not told that Council has been asked to charge before, and it does not address the $500,000 a year for sporting fields recorded in an earlier rate rise engagement.
10. It does not address the factors Council must consider under section 610D(1), including the importance of the service to the community, or the sportsground objectives in section 36F.
3. Questions for the Chief Executive
11. Why does the paper rescind MIN18.271 and not MIN23.43 and clause 3.2.b of POL18/70? If the recommendations are carried, what is the legal status of those two decisions?
12. How can Recommendation 2 reinstate the fees before the public notice and consideration of submissions in Recommendation 4, given section 610F(1)?
13. What is the net revenue after exemptions, Bookable administration, invoicing and bad debts?
14. Are the 16 section 355 committee sites included? Who pays their electricity, and who would keep any fee?
15. Which grant program, amount, co-contribution and deadline does the saved money relate to? Has Faster Assessments Round 2 or Better Open Spaces been assessed?
16. Where is the benchmarking against comparable councils that the paper promises? Which councils charge clubs for lighting, and which do not?
17. What would a typical senior and junior team pay across a season? What participation risk was assessed?
18. Why were clubs and the Sports Board not consulted before the paper was written?
19. The 2018 electricity bill was reported at $69,472 and the paper's figure is $29,480. What explains the difference, and is it LED and controller savings?
20. Was the paper checked against the live resolutions and the $500,000 a year for sporting fields recorded in an earlier rate rise engagement? If so, why are they not mentioned?
21. Would a narrower option, such as a fee for commercial and professional users only, as MIN23.43 asked the Sports Board to report on, have been considered and why was it not put?
4. Clearance of papers
Mr Constance, I have raised the standard of clearance of papers with you before. This paper shows that it has not been fixed. A paper that rescinds a spent resolution, leaves the live decisions untouched, reverses the statutory notice process and promises benchmarking it does not provide should not reach Council. I ask you again to improve your clearance procedures so that, before any paper goes to Councillors, it is checked for:
• every live resolution and policy on the same subject, including those that conflict with the recommendation;
• the legislation the recommendations rely on, checked against the Act itself;
• financial figures reconciled to their sources, with the net position stated;
• evidence for every claim made in support of the recommendation, including any promised benchmarking;
• the consultation and notice steps, in the order the law requires; and
• the history of the issue, including earlier decisions and commitments.
5. What I ask of Councillors
Vote against the recommendations. If you will not, move or support a motion that Council:
22. does not adopt the recommendations of CL26.309;
23. requests a revised report to a future meeting;
24. requires that report to include the matters in the questions above, with club and Sports Board consultation first and the section 610F notice before any fee is determined; and
25. retains MIN23.43 and clause 3.2.b of POL18/70 until Council resolves otherwise.
I have not verified the Code of Meeting Practice requirements for a motion of this kind. The Governance team can advise on the wording.
Councillors' contact details are on the Mayor and Councillors page: https://www.shoalhaven.nsw.gov.au/Council/What-guides-us/Leadership/Mayor-and-Councillors
Yours sincerely,
Stephen Prothero
Independent community journalist | Publisher, Eye on Shoalhaven Council
