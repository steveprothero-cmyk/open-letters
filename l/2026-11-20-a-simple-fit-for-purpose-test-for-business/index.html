An eight-question fit-for-purpose check for Shoalhaven council business papers

Mr Constance, Mayor and Councillors,

Bottom line: one eight-question check, used by the author before a paper is lodged and by a councillor before the vote, would lift the quality of business papers and give councillors a plain way to ask for what is missing.

  • For the CEO: the check is a way to test papers before they reach the agenda.
  • For councillors: the same eight questions can be put on notice or at a briefing when a paper leaves out something essential.
  • For my reporting: I will comment on the quality of the papers at each meeting.

Why I am writing
Last week I read the 24 November Ordinary Meeting agenda against the first Briefings Committee, held on 19 November. Of the five items I examined, two had gaps that the briefing exposed and the paper did not address, two were sound, and one I could only partly assess. The five were chosen by councillors' questions, so they are not a random sample. My conclusion was that the papers are uneven, not uniformly poor.

This letter is a criticism of the Chief Executive or but not of his Directors, the need leadership and guidance.

The problem I see is one of process. Papers that meet councillors' needs do so because someone asks the right questions before the paper goes out.

At present there is no visible test, so quality depends on who wrote the paper and who cleared it.

The Local Government Act 1993, section 335(f), lists among the general manager's functions ensuring that the mayor and other councillors are given timely information and advice necessary to effectively discharge their functions.

The Office of Local Government's Guidelines for the Appointment and Oversight of General Managers (2022), attached to item MM26.12 on the 27 October agenda, say the general manager should

ensure business papers contain sufficient information to allow councillors to make informed decisions. A test applied to every paper is a practical way to meet that.

The test
The graphic below is the check. It has eight questions with a Yes and No box each. Any "No" means the paper is not yet fit for purpose.

Title: Fit for purpose check - Description: Eight-question check for council business papers: decision, numbers, ceiling, purpose, law, baseline, consistency, complete.

PDF attached.
To the Chief Executive
The check is short enough to sit on the front of a paper's clearing sheet. The author ticks it before lodging, and the clearing officer is shown the result. A paper with a "No" goes back before it reaches the agenda.

That is cheaper than a councillor finding the gap at a briefing or, worse, after the vote.

That is a legal risk to Council, and ratepayers.

I will comment on the quality of the papers at each meeting in my reporting.

I would rather report improvement than gaps.

To Councillors
You can use the same check in questioning. If a paper leaves out an essential element, for example

the legal step,
the numbers behind a saving, or
what happens to money said to be set aside for a purpose, the question can be framed as one of the eight.
You can ask it on notice before the meeting, raise it at a briefing, move an amendment, or move to defer the item until it is fixed.

Questions
Question 1. Is there any reason Council will not trial the eight-question check on the papers for the next Ordinary Meeting?

Question 2. Is there any reason the result of the check would not be shown on the front of each paper, so that councillors can see it?

Question 3. Is there any reason the clearing officer would not confirm, on each paper, that the check has been done?
