Mr Constance,

The papers for the 24 November Ordinary Meeting contain a great deal of financial material.

A resident or a councillor who wants to know one thing,

“Is Council in better financial shape, and what did it cost?”, has to assemble the answer from the Annual Report, the audited financial statements, the Audit Office letter, the executive summary, the quarterly review and its attachments.

I have done that.

This letter sets out the answer I reached,

asks whether it is right, and
asks why the papers do not say it plainly.
1. The simple message, as I read it
This section is my own reading of the Council's figures. Every number is from the 24 November Attachments pack.

  • The consolidated result is a real improvement. The audited 2025/26 surplus before capital grants is $16.1M, against a $1.2M deficit the year before.
  • The General Fund, which pays for everyday services, is still in deficit. It lost $6.2M in 2025/26 and is forecast to lose $12.3M in 2026/27.
  • Ratepayers and grants paid for the improvement. Rates and annual charges rose $18.8M (9.8%) and operating grants rose $12.6M. Employee costs rose $3.5M.
  • Some of the improvement may not repeat. About $5M came from prepaying more of next year's Financial Assistance Grant, interest was $3.4M above budget, and employee costs were $9.6M under budget while 123 of 1,141.67 budgeted positions are vacant.
  • The asset base paid. The audited renewals ratio is 59.07%, maintenance 84.13% and the backlog 4.11% ($168.9M). All three worsened and none meets its benchmark. The audited notes say renewals were constrained in order to better improve the financial position.
  • The cash rise is mostly a change of label. Total cash is forecast to go from $295.2M to $296.9M. Unrestricted cash rises $28.7M, and internal reserves fall $20.2M.

Put in one sentence: the recovery is real, it has so far been paid for by ratepayers, by grant timing and by deferring renewal of assets, and the General Fund is not yet fixed.

Several things in the pack are stated plainly and deserve credit.

The Audit Office gave an unmodified opinion.
The draft Annual Report says the General Fund did not meet the benchmarks while the consolidated result did.
The audited commentary on assets is candid.
The executive summary says the structural challenge remains.
2. Why the message is hard to find
The Attachments pack runs to 1,056 pages.

The material needed to reach the message above sits in several places:

the draft Annual Report (pp. 3-104),
the audited statements and Audit Office letter (pp. 217-336),
the executive summary (pp. 337-345) and
the quarterly review attachments (pp. 476-500).
The audited asset ratios that fail their benchmarks are in the audited statements and the Report on Infrastructure Assets (pp. 334-336), well away from the General Fund and cash tables in the quarterly review (pp. 482, 494-495).
[Opinion] My view is that a councillor should be able to see the position in five minutes, and from the papers as presented that is not possible.

Section 8B of the Local Government Act 1993 says council spending should be responsible and sustainable, aligning general revenue and expenses, and that councils should have sound processes for asset maintenance and enhancement.

A reader cannot test Council against that standard if the General Fund result, the asset ratios and the cash position are not together on one page.
3. Questions
Questions 1 to 3 concern the papers. Questions 4 to 7 concern the figures. Each can be answered with a yes, a no or a single figure.

1. Question 1. Is the simple message in section 1 accurate, yes or no? If not, which sentence is wrong, and what is the correct statement?
2. Question 2. Why do the 24 November papers not contain a plain one-page statement of the position along those lines, and who decides how the financial papers are structured?
3. Question 3. Will future quarterly papers open with one page that gives the General Fund result, a five-quarter trend, the three audited asset ratios and total cash, before any consolidated figure, yes or no?
4. Question 4. The 2025/26 General Fund deficit was $6.2M and the 2026/27 forecast is $12.3M. With the extra grant prepayment (about $5M) removed from 2025/26, are the two figures comparable, yes or no?
5. Question 5. Audited renewals were 59.07%. The September paper forecasts 86.93% for 2026/27. What specific change produces that increase, and in which year does Council forecast reaching 100%?
6. Question 6. Total cash is forecast to move from $295.2M to $296.9M while unrestricted cash rises $28.7M. Can you confirm that $20.2M of that rise is the fall in internal reserves, and what the remainder is?
7. Question 7. Which services hold the 123 vacant positions, and what saving is built into the 2026/27 General Fund result?
I ask for answers before the meeting on Tuesday 24 November 2026 so that they can inform the debate. If any question cannot be answered, please say which and why, so the reason can be reported accurately.
