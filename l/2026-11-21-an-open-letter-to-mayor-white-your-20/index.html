Stephen Prothero · Eye on Shoalhaven Council · 21 November 2026 ·

Dear Mayor White,

Dear Councillors,

On 20 November 2026 Council published on its website a media release headed “Mayor White calls on Liza Butler MP to please explain”.

It demanded that a Member of the NSW Parliament apologise. It told the public that

“our council opposes this proposal on the basis that wildfires would be worse, the loss of timber jobs and the need to maintain access to our forested estate”.

And it pointed to

“a council resolution against Ms Butlers proposal for such a large and new Great Southern National Park”.

I have read the signed minutes of the meeting that appears to be the source of that resolution.

They do not say what the release says they say.

I am writing to the Mayor, in public, to ask her to explain, to correct the record, and to say why she should not be censured.

I am also writing to every Councillor, because the statement sits on Council’s website in Council’s name and each of you has a duty in relation to it.

1. What Council actually resolved
The only resolution I can find that fits is item CL26.65, Notice of Motion “Protecting Communities right to access Community Areas and Protect Our Logging Workers”, resolved as MIN26.141 at the Ordinary Meeting of 26 May 2026.

The minutes were confirmed on 29 June 2026. Cr Steele moved it. You seconded it. It carried 8 votes to 5.
It is about the Brooman State Forest.
It asks Council to write a supporting letter on community access and loggers’ job stability.
It asks Forestry Corporation to consider a boardwalk and viewing platform at Big Spotty.
It sets out what should happen if the State ever decides to wind up logging in State forests.
It maintains the forestry zoning, and Council “explicitly refuses to support any rezoning to environmental conservation” for two compartments.
It asks that any tenure change be developed with the RFS.
The motion does not contain the words “national park”.

It does not mention the Great Southern Forest or the National Parks Association.

It does not mention Ms Butler.

It does not mention wildfire or bushfire.

I searched all 25 pages of the minutes of that meeting.

The nearest text is clause 7, which asks that any State Forest or Crown Lands tenure change be developed in formal partnership with the RFS, with written concurrence that no gates or road closures will impede heavy tanker access. That is a safeguard condition. It is not a finding that wildfires would be worse.

The motion also pre-dates the public launch of the Great Southern Forest proposal on 17 June 2026. I do not rely on timing alone. The text speaks for itself.

2. What the release says, and where it departs from the record
• “A council resolution against Ms Butlers proposal for such a large and new Great Southern National Park.” MIN26.141 is not a resolution about a national park, and it is not against any proposal by name. The proposal is the National Parks Association’s. Ms Butler told 2ST on 21 November that she hosted the NPA at Parliament, that the NPA asked to bring its supporters, and that she presented nothing. Your release says the list was one “that she presented”. It does not say what you rely on for that.
• “Our council opposes this proposal on the basis that wildfires would be worse.” No Council resolution says this. The words do not appear in MIN26.141.
• “Without a council resolution and without the knowledge or consent of the elected body.” This asks Council to treat the absence of a resolution to support the proposal as a wrong done by Ms Butler. Council has also made no resolution to oppose it. Holiday Haven is managed by the Chief Executive Officer, who is responsible for its day-to-day management. The person who can say who signed for Holiday Haven, and with what authority, is the CEO.
3. The standard that applies
Section 226(c) of the Local Government Act 1993 makes the mayor the principal member and spokesperson of the governing body, “including representing the views of the council as to its local priorities”.

Section 232(1)(f) says that a councillor’s role includes “to uphold and represent accurately the policies and decisions of the governing body”. That applies to you as a councillor, and it applies to each of you.

Council’s adopted Code of Conduct (POL24/134) is more specific.

A press release is “public comment” (cl 3.26). The Mayor and the CEO are Council’s official spokespersons (cl 3.27). When you comment as an official representative of Council, you must defend and uphold Council’s corporate and resolved position (cl 3.29(a)).

You must not make a comment that will bring Council into disrepute (cl 3.29(b)). And “you must not make any comment that contains content about the Council that is misleading or deceptive” (cl 3.29(f)).

If the release is said to be personal comment, cl 3.30(c) requires that personal public comment be made honestly and not be misleading to the public.

I make no allegation about your intent. Clause 3.29(f) is about content.

The question is whether a statement that Council has resolved to oppose this proposal, because wildfires would be worse, is accurate when no resolution says so. I do not think it is.

You are in the best position to say what you relied on, and I ask you to say it.

4. Council resources
Part 8 of the Code requires Council resources to be used ethically, effectively, efficiently and carefully in the course of official functions. It restricts the use of Council’s official material, including its letterhead, email and social media, to official Council business.

The release was published on Council’s website and carries Council’s name.
Its subject is a proposal by an outside organisation, a Labor MP’s hosting of a function in State Parliament, and a demand for an apology, months before a State election.
Council has no resolved position on that proposal.
Council has a legitimate interest in one narrow question: how a Council-run holiday park business came to be on a supporters list. That question could have been answered by an email to the CEO and a call to the National Parks Association. It did not need a public demand on Council’s website.

In 2024 you led a campaign that promised to eliminate big party politics and block voting in council. A release that puts Council’s name to a political attack on a Labor MP, on a State-level issue, is difficult to reconcile with that.

I ask whether Council’s website, Council’s media function and Council officers’ time were used to prepare and publish this release, and on what authority.

5. Questions
Please answer each of these in writing by 5pm on Friday 4 December 2026.

• Which resolution of Council do you say records that “our council opposes this proposal”? Please give the resolution number and date.
• Where in MIN26.141 do the words “wildfire” or “national park” appear?
• What is the basis for the statement that Ms Butler “presented” the list?
• Who drafted the release? Who approved it? Which Council officers worked on it, and for how long? Was the CEO consulted before it was published? Under which Council policy was it issued?
• Has your office, or any Council officer, given information about Holiday Haven’s inclusion on the list to any Member of Parliament or their staff, before or after 20 November? When did you first learn of it, and from whom?
• Who at Holiday Haven signed the National Parks Association statement, on what date, and with what authority? What did Council management do on learning of it?
• Will you withdraw the statement that Council opposes the proposal on the basis that wildfires would be worse, correct the release on Council’s website, and apologise to Ms Butler for the demand that she apologise?
• If you will not, why should Councillors not be asked to censure you under section 440G of the Act?
6. To the Councillors
Eight of you voted for MIN26.141. Five voted against. All thirteen of you know what it says. Section 232(1)(f) puts the same duty on each of you that it puts on the Mayor: to uphold and represent accurately the policies and decisions of the governing body. A statement on Council’s website that Council has resolved something it has not resolved misrepresents a decision you made, or opposed, and it will stand in Council’s name until someone corrects it.

I ask you to do three things.

• First, ask the Mayor, in writing and now, to correct the release.
• Second, if she does not do so by 4 December 2026, move a notice of motion under Council’s Code of Meeting Practice to formally censure her.
• Third, if you are not yet satisfied that the conduct meets the threshold, resolve to refer the allegation to the Departmental Chief Executive under section 440H(2)(c) of the Act, so that an independent body can decide.
The law is this. Section 440G(1) provides that a council may by resolution at a meeting formally censure a councillor for misconduct.

Under section 440G(3) it may do so only if it is satisfied that the councillor has engaged in misconduct on one or more occasions.

Under section 440G(4) the resolution must specify the grounds.

Section 440F(1) defines misconduct to include a contravention of the Act and a failure to comply with an applicable requirement of a code of conduct.

The grounds I suggest are these:

• failure to comply with clauses 3.29(a), 3.29(b) and 3.29(f) of the Code, in that the release of 20 November 2026 stated that Council had resolved to oppose the Great Southern Forest proposal on the basis that wildfires would be worse, when Council’s resolution MIN26.141 does not say so; and
• failure to comply with Part 8 of the Code, in that Council’s resources and official channels were used to publish a statement on a matter on which Council has no resolved position.
A motion in that form could read:

“That Council, being satisfied that Cr White engaged in misconduct within the meaning of section 440F(1)(b) of the Local Government Act 1993 on the grounds set out in the notice of motion, formally censure Cr White under section 440G, and direct that the release of 20 November 2026 be corrected on Council’s website.”

The Mayor should be given the chance to respond before any vote, which is why this letter gives her the questions above first. I also ask you to consider whether the Mayor should vacate the chair for that item.

A censure is a formal expression of disapproval. It does not remove anyone from office, and it carries no fine. The only thing it asks is that Council say, on the record, that its name should not be put to a statement the record does not support.

Please address replies to me at the contact details Council holds. I will publish the replies, or the absence of them.

Yours sincerely,

Stephen Prothero
